# Manuscript 11 Eq. (50): certified local radii and distributed 25k pilot

Source: 30-page PDF SHA-256 `266cd62eddf952d4535a1964a7861b109f2a08d991e82d72dcd5f3c087a695b1`. Merged #399 achieved 10,758 measured relative bits (CI 44323287771). This experiment targets 25,000 relative bits at 40,000 working bits; neither target is the requested million-digit final certification.

The proof of the inverse charts from #399 is retained, with a refinement. Partition the real x=theta/pi interval into eight dyadic bands [j/8,(j+1)/8]. For each curve and each band, use the same 1/128 complex tiling and six nested extension widths d=(1/8,3/16,1/4,5/16,3/8,7/16). Each tile is assigned to its smallest covering extension rectangle for each band. The lower endpoint of Re H is accumulated by exact comparison, and cumulative minima give valid bounds c_j on every nested rectangle. All tiles also verify Re F>0, |H|<4 and |K|<12. The radial bound sum c_j*(d_j-d_{j-1}), divided by 3, exceeds a downward-rounded dyadic R individually for each band and curve (ell<3). The exact comparison is checked; the floating-point candidate does not prove the inequality. As in #399, the strictly positive real part of H implies injectivity of the primitive, and Rouche gives an inverse disk of radius R centered at every real normalized arclength argument whose angle belongs to the band. The angle is bounded by 5 and curvature by 12 in these disks. The Eulerian band radii range from 57/256 to 109/256; the uniform 7/32 bound remains available for translation errors.

At each real shifted midpoint, use the minimum radius among all bands that could contain its exact angle divided by pi and both curves. Choose an even Taylor order p by the explicit inequality 5*(step/R)^p/(1-step/R) < 2^(-target-512). The two-sided phase continuation and squared curvature integration retain the tails and the additive phase errors of #399. Only the local jet bounds use the improved radius; the comparison with the original unshifted chart uses the independently proved uniform radius. The first midpoint uses the sharper radius at the exact endpoint. Arithmetic precision scheduling is a performance heuristic only; all operations are enclosing and the final exact endpoint/relative-bit guard is mandatory.

Two independent runners certify the two halves of the integral, each starting from the exact endpoint zero or pi. No assumption of agreement is used as a proof: each half has its own enclosing interval. The final job verifies identical parameters, distinct directions and panel counts, positive integrals and compatible length enclosures, reconstructs enclosing balls with exact endpoint guards, sums and takes the square root. It checks the measured relative accuracy and emits the full exact dyadic interval. The small local pilot at 4,096 working bits, target 1,000, passed with 1,503 relative bits. The independent-file mode and recombination were also tested at 6,000 working bits, target 2,000, and passed with 2,505 relative bits. No manuscript or historical certificate is changed.
