# M16 §9.5 — geometric realization: spectral, heat and complexification bridge

Scope: M16 only. Base 2e94915a4e1c28a77fcf64ad8a4b1789b59d651f (#668 merged). Candidate, not certified before exact-head CI. See the earlier geometric bridge note M16_krein_geometric_bridge_20261127.md.

## 1. Geometric eigenspaces and Hilbert bases
For the round unit sphere S³ with its canonical spin structure, the classical elliptic spectral theorem gives smooth complete eigensections for the self-adjoint Hodge and Dirac realizations. Use the coclosed one-form eigenspaces with eigenvalues (n+2)^2 and real multiplicities 2((n+2)^2−1); no zero branch since H¹(S³)=0. Use signed Dirac eigenspaces ±(n+3/2), each of complex dimension (n+1)(n+2), as in Bär (1996) and Ikeda–Taniguchi (1978), references in the preceding note. Choose a finite orthonormal basis in each eigenspace and take the union. Distinct eigenspaces are orthogonal by symmetry, and their span is dense by compact-resolvent spectral completeness. The construction of the indexed Hilbert basis from an orthonormal family with dense span is now an explicit Lean candidate using Mathlib HilbertBasis.mk. The existence and smoothness of the geometric eigensections and the stated multiplicities are still classical inputs, not constructed in Lean on S³.

For the realification of complex spinors followed by complexification, both the original and conjugate eigenspaces occur; the multiplicities double. The absolute lower bound 3/2 is unchanged. The new generic resolvent theorem uses arbitrary index types and real eigenvalues, avoiding the incorrect undoubled complexified spectrum. It derives the resolvent on the original Hilbert space from the closed symmetric graph and complete eigenfamily, without assuming graph equality or the original-space resolvent.

## 2. Heat kernel
For each real coclosed eigenspace, write its evaluation map at x in an orthonormal fibre frame as a finite real matrix. An isometry gives an orthogonal transformation of the eigenspace (it preserves the L² pairing and commutes with the Hodge Laplacian and codifferential), and an orthogonal transformation of the fibre. Sum of squared coordinates is invariant under both orthogonal transformations. The new module proves orthogonal_sum_sq, fibre_evaluation_invariant and eigenspace_diagonal_constant, with the coordinate transformation hypothesis made explicit. On S³, transitivity therefore gives constant evaluation density. Integration yields m_n/Vol(S³), and for positive epsilon the exponentially weighted series converges by Gaussian decay. Finite sums converge uniformly in x from the Cauchy–Schwarz estimate and the Gaussian tail; hence the restricted heat evaluation norm is bounded by sqrt(sum_n m_n exp(-2 epsilon (n+2)^2)/Vol(S³)). The actual isometry action on differential forms, its intertwining property, the fibre transformation and the global uniform convergence estimate have not been kernel-checked. Invariance of the full two-point heat kernel follows classically from intertwining and uniqueness of the heat semigroup; the diagonal is the quantity used in the coupling bound.

## 3. Unbounded complexification
For a real operator graph G, define the complexification graph by independent real and imaginary component membership. Prove it is closed if G is closed, single-valued if G is single-valued, its domain is Dom(G) × Dom(G), and its entire adjoint graph (including domain) is the componentwise complexification of the real adjoint graph. The complex pairing is the real/imaginary formula from #645. Consequently real self-adjointness gives equality of the two complexified graphs. This is a graph-level construction in the product topology; it does not yet install a full complex Hilbert-space instance with Euclidean product norm, prove completeness of that instance or establish its operator norm/compactness equivalence. The classical density argument is that Dom(G) dense implies Dom(G) × Dom(G) dense in the product topology.

## 4. Application
eigenfamily_block_disk derives the block resolvent on the original Hilbert spaces under complete orthonormal eigenfamilies of the closed symmetric graphs, spectral bounds 4 and 3/2, bounded diagonal perturbations VA, VD and norm relation ||X|| ≤ 2||Y||. It combines #668 with the already certified graph perturbation and block Schur resolvent theorems in #645. It is not an unconditional theorem on geometric spinor/one-form L² spaces.
