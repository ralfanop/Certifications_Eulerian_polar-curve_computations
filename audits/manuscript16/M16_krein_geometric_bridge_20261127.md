# M16 §9.5 — geometric bridge and scope (2026-11-27)

This note provides the classical geometric proof underlying the new Lean candidates. It is not an assertion that the differential-geometric input is kernel-checked. The round unit sphere and its canonical spin structure are used throughout.

## 1. Unitary spectral identification including domains
Let H_F=L²(S³;Σ), H_B=ker δ in L² one-forms (distributional coclosedness). For the spatial Dirac operator D₃ and the transverse Hodge Laplacian Δ_T use the classical self-adjoint elliptic realizations. Their smooth eigensections form complete orthonormal systems. D₃ has eigenvalues ±(n+3/2), multiplicity (n+1)(n+2) for each sign; Δ_T has eigenvalues (n+2)², multiplicity 2((n+2)²−1), n≥0. H¹_dR(S³)=0, so there is no harmonic one-form branch. References: C. Bär, The Dirac operator on space forms of positive curvature, J. Math. Soc. Japan 48 (1996), 69–83, https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/04810069; A. Ikeda and Y. Taniguchi, Spectra and eigenforms of the Laplacian on Sⁿ and Pⁿ(C), Osaka J. Math. 15 (1978), 515–546, https://doi.org/10.18910/6956.

For either complete eigenbasis e_j with real eigenvalues λ_j, define Ux=(⟨e_j,x⟩)_j (inner product linear in the second argument). Parseval proves isometry, and summing finite coefficient truncations proves surjectivity. If x is in Dom(T), symmetry gives (UTx)_j=λ_j(Ux)_j. Conversely, if λ_j(Ux)_j is square summable, finite truncations x_N converge to x and T x_N converge to U⁻¹(λ_j(Ux)_j); closedness proves x∈Dom(T) and the asserted action. This identifies the graphs, not just eigenvalues. In Lean eigenbasis_graph_transport checks the final graph identification given the full spectral graph relation; it does not construct the geometric eigensections or prove the elliptic spectral theorem.

## 2. Clifford multiplication and the heat estimate
Identify S³ with SU(2), take a global left-invariant orthonormal frame for the round metric and its spin lift. The spinor bundle is trivialized with fibre C². With a consistent orientation and sign convention, Γ(β)=−i c(β) is the Hermitian Pauli contraction for a real covector β=(a,b,c):
Γ(β)=[[c,a−ib],[a+ib,−c]].
Its square is |β|²I and its Hermitian adjoint is itself; hence |Γ(β)ψ|²=|β|²|ψ|². This supplies the effective fibre multiplication, without identifying a real-linear current derivative with a complex-linear derivative.

Let E_ε=exp(−εΔ₁), ε>0, and restrict it to the coclosed sector; it commutes with P_T. For each transverse eigenlevel n the sum of squared pointwise norms of an orthonormal basis of that eigenspace is invariant under the transitive round isometry group, since the group acts unitarily within the eigenspace. Its integral equals the dimension m_n, so its constant value is m_n/Vol(S³). For a transverse a and a finite heat expansion, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
|E_ε a(x)|² ≤ ||a||²₂ [Σ_n m_n exp(−2ε(n+2)²)/Vol(S³)].
The series converges (polynomial times Gaussian); the same tail bound gives uniform convergence and passage to L∞. Thus the restricted heat norm is at most the square root of the displayed sum. For the full one-form heat operator one must also include the exact-form branch; the same argument applies (its eigenvalues and multiplicities have polynomial growth). The manuscript's full H_ε is an upper bound for the restricted norm. Norm convergence of finite spectral cutoffs in L² follows from the exponential supremum tail, proving compactness. No assertion that the heat operator at ε=0 is compact.

## 3. Effective couplings
Define Mβ=Γ(β)ψ₀ on real one-forms and the underlying real spinor space. Pointwise multiplication satisfies |Mβ|=|β||ψ₀|. Its bounded L² extension follows from smoothness of ψ₀ on compact S³, with ||M||≤||ψ₀||∞. In real pairings (M†φ)_j=Re⟨φ,Γ_j ψ₀⟩, hence (dj)_{ψ₀}φ=2e M†φ. This follows by differentiating the quadratic current with a real parameter and Hermiticity of Γ_j. Let J: H_B→L²Ω¹ be the isometric inclusion and J†=P_T. Then
Y=e M E_ε J,
X=−2e J† E_ε M†.
Self-adjointness of E_ε gives X=−2Y†, hence ||X||=2||Y||. Both compositions are compact for positive ε. The integral estimate
||Y a||²₂=e²∫|E_ε Ja|² |ψ₀|² ≤ e² H_ε²||a||²₂||ψ₀||²₂
gives (147); for each current component the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives |(dj φ)_j|≤2|e||φ||ψ₀|. Summing three squares and using the contractive projection and heat gives (146). These are bounds, not equalities for the operator norms. The Lean candidate integral_square_domination proves a measurable density estimate; clifford_integral_bound specializes it to the explicit Pauli contraction. The global geometric operators, projection, and heat kernel estimates are still classical inputs, not declarations proved on S³ in Lean.

## 4. Complexification
For a real Hilbert space H_R, let H_C=H_R⊕iH_R with complex scalar multiplication (a+ib)(x+iy)=(ax−by)+i(bx+ay), inner product ⟨x+iy,u+iv⟩=(x,u)+(y,v)+i[(x,v)−(y,u)], and squared norm ||x||²+||y||². For bounded real-linear T, T_C(x+iy)=Tx+iTy is complex linear and ||T_C||=||T|| (upper bound by the sum of squares, lower bound on y=0). Direct expansion shows (T_C)*=(T*)_C. If T is compact, a bounded sequence has a subsequence with convergence of both transformed components by two successive extractions. For a densely defined closed real operator, complexify its domain as Dom(T)⊕iDom(T); the graph is closed by componentwise closedness, and the adjoint domain is exactly the complexification of the real adjoint domain, by testing on x+i0 and 0+iy. Consequently real self-adjointness and the weighted coupling identity are preserved. The original complex spinor Hilbert space regarded as real and then complexified has both a copy and a conjugate copy; do not silently use the undoubled Dirac multiplicities for that complexification. The spectral lower bound is unchanged. Lean candidates check scalar-linearity, additivity, squared norm upper bound and adjoint pairing for explicit pairs, not the entire Hilbert-space instance or the closed-operator complexification.
