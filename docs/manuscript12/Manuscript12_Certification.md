# Manuscript 12: Lean 4 and Arb certification record

*Bargmann–Fock Representations of the Eulerian Polar Curve: Polyanalytic Extensions and Lanczos Asymptotics*. Roberto Alfano, 20 November 2026. A PDF version of this record is [Manuscript12_Certification.pdf](Manuscript12_Certification.pdf).

## 1. Scope.

This record states, for manuscript 12, which results are kernel-checked in Lean 4 \[5\], which values are certified in Arb ball arithmetic \[3\] and how the libraries are rebuilt and audited. It replaces Appendices B, C and D of the article (Lean 4 certification) and the description of the supplementary packages formerly given in its Data Availability statement. Equation, section and result numbers are those of the article that cites this record; in it the subsections of Section 5 are numbered 5.1–5.8. All directories named below are in the repository <https://github.com/ralfanop/Certifications_Eulerian_polar-curve_computations>.

The Fourier–Laplace analysis of Lemma 5.5 is formalised in the library `Bridge` (below), whose theorem `lemma_5_5_phi` proves the bound $|\varphi_M'-I'|\leq C/M$. `UniformLanczos` is also compiled with that toolchain in the Eulerian–Grothendieck project, where the module `Joined` inserts this bound into `endpoint_concrete`, so that Lemma 5.7(b) holds for the actual $\varphi_M$ (theorem `endpoint_actual`), and the module `Profile` proves Lemma 5.7(a), from the conclusions of Lemma 5.6, and Corollary 5.10.

On 20 November 2026 eight modules with 115 theorems were added to the Eulerian–Grothendieck project: `RightTail` and `LogConcave` in `Bridge/Saddle`, and `Lemma56`, `Lemma56Endpoint`, `Lemma56SmallM`, `Lemma58`, `Lemma58Uniform` and `Theorem59` in its copy of `UniformLanczos`; they are in the directory [`lean/EulerianGrothendieck`](https://github.com/ralfanop/Certifications_Eulerian_polar-curve_computations/tree/main/lean/EulerianGrothendieck). For the actual $\varphi_M$ they prove Lemma 5.6, Lemma 5.7(a) for $M\geq3$ and Lemma 5.8, and they prove Theorem 5.9 from one explicit hypothesis, the estimate (105) of Levin and Lubinsky \[4\]. They certify the following statements.

- Lemma 5.6 for $M\geq3$: $\varphi_M'$ is odd, continuous and strictly increasing on $(-1/2,1/2)$, with $\varphi_M'(0)=0$ (module `Lemma56`, theorems `lemma_5_6_strictMono` and `lemma_5_6_continuousOn`). The Lean proof does not use the theorem of Floater \[1\] quoted in the article. The module `LogConcave` proves instead, by induction on $M$ through the identity $w_{M+1}'(y)=w_M(y+1/2)-w_M(y-1/2)$ for $M\geq2$ (`hasDerivAt_w_succ`), that $w_M>0$ on $(-M/2,M/2)$ for $M\geq2$ (`w_pos`) and that $w_M'/w_M$ is strictly decreasing there for $M\geq3$ (`w_logDeriv_strictAnti`).

- The end interval in Lemma 5.6: $w_M(M/2-\delta)=\delta^{M-1}/(M-1)!$ for $M\geq1$ and $0\leq\delta<1$ (module `RightTail`, theorem `w_right_tail`); hence, for $M\geq3$, $\varphi_M'(x)=(M-1)/[M(1/2-x)]$ for $1/2-1/M<x<1/2$, and $x\varphi_M'(x)\geq c_M/(1/2-x)$ for $1/2-1/(2M)\leq x<1/2$ with $c_M=[1/2-1/(2M)](M-1)/M$ (module `Lemma56Endpoint`, theorem `lemma_5_6_blowup`).

- The cases $M=2$ and $M=1$ of Lemma 5.6 (module `Lemma56SmallM`): $\varphi_1'=0$ on $(-1/2,1/2)$; $\varphi_2'(x)=1/(1-2x)$ for $0<x<1/2$ and $\varphi_2'(x)=-1/(1+2x)$ for $-1/2<x<0$; and $x\varphi_2'(x)=|x|/(1-2|x|)$ on $(-1/2,1/2)$ (`lemma_5_6_two`). For $0\leq x<1/2$ this closed form equals $(x/2)/(1/2-x)$; the lower bound of the lemma for $M=2$ is read off it and is not a separate Lean statement.

- Lemma 5.7(a) for the actual $\varphi_M$ and every $M\geq3$, with no hypothesis: for every $t>0$ the equation $\Phi_M(\xi)=t$ has a root in $(0,1/2)$, which is its only root in $[0,1/2)$ (`lemma_5_7a_actual`). For $M=2$ and $M=1$ the theorems `lemma_5_7a` and `lemma_5_7a_one` of `Profile` are stated for a general integrand; their application to the closed forms of the previous item is not a separate Lean theorem.

- Lemma 5.8 for the actual weights (135), with $Q_{j,M}$ of (136) on $\mathcal I_{j,M}$ (modules `Lemma58` and `Lemma58Uniform`): the derivative (137) (`hasDerivAt_QjM`); condition (i) at both endpoints of $\mathcal I_{j,M}$, for $M\geq2$ (`lemma_5_8_i`); condition (ii) for $M\geq3$; the identity (138), namely that the first Mhaskar–Rakhmanov–Saff equation, in the $\theta$-form of (140), holds if and only if $\Phi_M(\xi)=j/M$, while the second holds by oddness (`mrs_first_actual`, `mrs_actual`); and the differentiability of $\varphi_M'$ on $(-1/2,1/2)$ for $M\geq4$. The theorem `lemma_5_8_uniform` joins them: for $t_1>0$ and $t_2$ there are $0<\underline a<b<1/2$, $r^*>1$ with $r^*b<1/2$, a constant $L\geq0$ and $M_0$ such that, for all $M\geq M_0$ and $j\geq1$ with $t_1\leq j/M\leq t_2$, the root $\xi_{j,M}$ of $\Phi_M(\xi)=j/M$ lies in $(\underline a,b)$ and is the only root in $[0,b]$, $[-r^*,r^*]\subset\mathcal I_{j,M}$, $Q_{j,M}'$ satisfies (ii) and the Lipschitz bound (139) with the constant $L$ on $[-r^*,r^*]$, hence (iii) with $\gamma_0=1/4$ and the constant $L(2r^*)^{3/4}$ and (iv) with $\gamma_1=3/4$ and the constant $L(2r^*)^{1/4}$, and both equations of (v) hold. These constants do not depend on $(j,M)$. The condition $M\geq M_0$ replaces $j\geq j_0$ of the article: $j/M\leq t_2$ and $j\geq t_2M_0$ give $M\geq M_0$.

- The input of Theorem 5.9 (module `Theorem59`). Definition 1.1 of \[4, p. 6\] is the structure `ClassQ`: weights $e^{-2nQ_n}$ on open intervals $\mathcal I_n=(c_n,d_n)$, $-\infty\leq c_n<d_n\leq\infty$, with $[-r^*,r^*]\subset\mathcal I_n$ and $r^*>1$, conditions (i)–(iv) as quoted in Subsection 5.1 of the article and condition (v) in the form (101)–(102), as Lebesgue integrals over $(-1,1)$. The coefficient $A_{n,m}$ of (103)–(104) is the nonnegative square root of $D_{m+2}D_m/D_{m+1}^2$, where $D_k$ is the Hankel determinant of order $k$ of the moments of $e^{-2nQ_n}$ on $\mathcal I_n$; for every monic orthogonal family $P_0,P_1,\dots$ of a moment functional with positive squared norms this quotient equals $\|P_{m+1}\|^2/\|P_m\|^2$, that is $\gamma_{n,m}^2/\gamma_{n,m+1}^2$ (`recSq_eq_norm_ratio`). The estimate (105) of Theorem 13.4 of \[4, p. 124\] is the proposition `LL_13_4`: for $\{Q_n\}\in\mathcal Q$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,1/3)$ there are $\tau_1>0$, $C$ and $n_0$ with $|A_{n,m}-1/2|\leq Cn^{-\tau_1}$ for all $n\geq n_0$ and $|m-n|\leq n^{1/3-\varepsilon}$. This published result is an input: it is the hypothesis of the Lean theorems of the next item and is not formalised, and neither is the classical existence of the orthonormal polynomials $p_{n,m}$.

- Theorem 5.9 under that hypothesis (module `Theorem59`), with $\beta_j^{[M]}=D_{j+1}D_{j-1}/D_j^2$, $j\geq1$, for the moments of the cardinal B-spline density $w_M$ of `Bridge/Saddle`. The moment of order $k$ of the weight (135) on $\mathcal I_{j,M}$ is $(M\xi)^{-k}$ times that of $w_M$ (`moment_QjM`), which gives the scaling (142) (`scaling_142`). The sequences of the proof, $Q_n=Q_{n,\nu(n)}$ for the admissible $n$ and $Q_n(x)=x^2$ on $\mathbb R$ for the others, belong to $\mathcal Q$ (`classQ_seq`), so that (105) with $m=n-1$ gives $A_{n,n-1}\to1/2$ (`LLA_tendsto`) and, by (133), $\mathcal E_{n,\nu(n)}\to0$ along every $\nu$ with $n/\nu(n)\in[t_1,t_2]$ for all large $n$ (`seq_limit`). For every compact $\mathcal K\subset(0,\infty)$ the limit (141) follows in two forms: for every prescribed tolerance there is a threshold such that $|\beta_j^{[M]}/M^2-F(j/M)|$ is below the tolerance for all $j$ at least the threshold and all $M$ with $j/M\in\mathcal K$ (`theorem_5_9`); and with the supremum, as printed (`theorem_5_9_sup`), the families whose suprema appear in (141) being bounded above from some index on (`theorem_5_9_bddAbove`). The identification of the moments of $w_M$ with the integral Hankel determinants of independent uniform sums used for Theorem 5.1 (Section 5 of this record) belongs to a separate Lean package and is not part of these modules.

## 3. Probabilistic asymptotics and scalar correlation geometry: the Eulerian–Grothendieck project.

The Lean 4 formalisation of this section comprises 744 theorems, the $418+286+40$ checked declarations of its three supplements. The supplementary Eulerian–Grothendieck project, dated 29 October 2026, records 418 checked declarations in 52 modules: 383 from the previous certificate and 35 additions. It uses Lean 4.24.0 and Mathlib commit `f897ebcf72cd16f89ab4577d0c826cd14afaafc7`; it is a project of the repository separate from the library `UniformLanczos`, which uses the newer toolchain stated above. The seven added modules were compiled; unchanged modules were matched by source and compiled-object hashes. A fresh audit of all 418 declarations found only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, with no custom axiom or proof placeholder. The portable script rebuilds all project modules by default.

A second supplement of the same date adds 19 new modules with 286 checked declarations, compiled one module at a time with the same toolchain and the 52 earlier modules untouched (re-verified by hash); its separate audit again found only the three standard axioms, and no `sorry`, `admit`, `axiom` or `native_decide`.

For the actual product-law integral in (203) and its reciprocal, Lean proves expansions at every fixed finite order $R$ and fixed $c>1/12$, with remainder $C_{c,R}M^{-R-1}$ for all integers $M\geq1$ (with a constant $C_{c,R}$ whose existence is certified; the printed constant (245) of Theorem 9.10 is certified for $M\geq4096$). All thirteen displayed rational coefficients are identified with the actual integral coefficients, including the $O(M^{-14})$ remainders. The norm remainder is also uniform for all $c\geq c_0>1/12$, with a constant depending only on $c_0$ and $R$. Thus the probabilistic part (262) of the first special-scale statement is formalised. The project also certifies the exact projector (268), attainment of its scalar maximum, and the global optimisation and critical limit (289) for the Eulerian phase.

The second supplement formalises, in addition: the Gaussian Fock identification of Theorem 9.2, with the vacuum probability as the squared norm of the projection onto the constants in $L^2$ of the Gaussian measure; the branch $0<c\leq1/12$ of Theorem 9.6; the constants of Lemmas 9.3–9.4 and Theorem 9.5; the majorant (240) and the printed all-order constant for $M\geq4096$; parameter-uniform reciprocal remainders; the Bernoulli and zeta identifications of the cumulants at every order; the arbitrary-phase form of Theorem 9.15 with the constant $2000$ for $0<p\leq1/144$; the sharp Lipschitz constant $2/\sqrt5$ on the disk; the bilinear scalar and vector interpretation with $q_{\mathbb C}(P)=4$ and $\mathcal Q\leq K$ for every admissible complex Grothendieck constant $K$; the exposure of the frame Gram matrix on the elliptope and the endpoint decompositions. Neither convergence of an infinite $1/M$ expansion nor uniformity at the critical threshold is asserted. The supplement contains reproducible programs, enclosures, sources, declaration types, build records and the axiom audit.

A third supplement, dated 2 November 2026, proves Lemma 9.8 at every order (module `ExactPolynomialAllOrders`, 40 theorems); the proportional-regime coverage of `UniformLanczos` includes all seven coefficients of (143).

## 4. Sections 3, 4, 6–8 and Lemma 5.5: the library `Bridge`.

The proofs of Sections 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8, of Subsections 9.1–9.2, of Lemma 5.5 and of Appendix A are checked in Lean 4 in the library `Bridge` of the Eulerian–Grothendieck project, in the directory [`lean/EulerianGrothendieck`](https://github.com/ralfanop/Certifications_Eulerian_polar-curve_computations/tree/main/lean/EulerianGrothendieck), with the same toolchain and Mathlib commit. The library, dated 2 November 2026, has 51 modules and 821 theorems; its audit file `AuditBridge.lean` finds only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, so no theorem depends on `sorry` or on a further axiom. The file [`README_Bridge_V173.md`](https://github.com/ralfanop/Certifications_Eulerian_polar-curve_computations/blob/main/lean/EulerianGrothendieck/README_Bridge_V173.md) of the same directory pairs the numbered statements and equations of these sections with their Lean theorems, in the numbering of the article. After installing the toolchain named in the file `lean-toolchain`, the commands `lake exe cache get`, `lake build Bridge` and `lake env lean AuditBridge.lean`, run in that directory, fetch the compiled Mathlib, rebuild the library and repeat the audit.

For Lemma 5.5, the twenty-one modules of `Bridge/Saddle` prove (115) with explicit first and second derivatives (theorem `lemma_5_5`), the continuity of the second derivative on $[-b,b]$ (`lemma_5_5_C2`) and the two consequences stated after (115) (`lemma_5_5_phi`). They depart from the printed proof in one point: the tilt $s$ is held fixed while differentiating in $y=Mx$, and $s=s(x)$ is set afterwards, which is legitimate because the Fourier representation of $w_M$ holds for every real tilt. The library also proves the decimal values displayed in (4)–(6), (12), (13) and (31), and $\eta(1/4)$ in (114). The modules `RightTail` and `LogConcave`, added to `Bridge/Saddle` on 20 November 2026 for Lemma 5.6, are described in Section 2; the root file `Bridge.lean` does not import them and they are not counted above.

## 5. Fixed-degree Lanczos coefficients: Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.2.

The Lean 4 formalisation of Theorem 5.1 has two principal formal statements, which cover, respectively, the integral Hankel determinants of actual independent uniform sums and the coefficients of a defining monic orthogonal recurrence. For each fixed positive degree they prove eventual nonvanishing of the required denominator and an existential quantitative bound $C_j/M$. The supplementary proof also verifies the exact moment-polynomial bridge for arbitrary independent uniform sums and moment orders, Gaussian Gram positivity, and the first determinant coefficients. Its archived build logs report successful checking with the toolchain of the Eulerian–Grothendieck project; the complete module names, revision identifier, axiom audit and a portable build script are in the directory [`lean/FixedDegreeLanczos`](https://github.com/ralfanop/Certifications_Eulerian_polar-curve_computations/tree/main/lean/FixedDegreeLanczos), with the exact Lanczos data and the archived sources and logs. On 2 November 2026 the repository’s continuous integration recompiled it from source, together with the proofs of Corollary 5.2 and of the exact values of $\beta_1^{[M]}$, $\beta_2^{[M]}$ and $\kappa_4(S_M)$ in Subsection 9.5. The independently checked rational expressions for degrees $5\leq j\leq16$ are included as exact supplementary data.

## 6. Numerical certificates.

Table 1 of the article compares $\beta_j^{[M]}/M^2$ with $F(j/M)$. The original Gauss–Legendre and Stieltjes computations are retained in [`numerics/uniform_lanczos`](https://github.com/ralfanop/Certifications_Eulerian_polar-curve_computations/tree/main/numerics/uniform_lanczos). The supplement of 29 October 2026 independently certifies all 25 displayed values and errors by Arb intervals: exact uniform-convolution moments determine the monic recurrence through parity Gram matrices or a modified Chebyshev recurrence, while validated integration encloses $F$. Every resulting enclosure lies inside its exact rational rounding cell. The approximate constancy of $M\mathcal E_{j,M}$ is consistent with the rate $O(1/M)$ that Theorem 5.14 proves analytically (Section 2); these computations do not prove it. The displayed saddlepoint value $\eta(1/4)\approx-0.184391$ is independently certified; this checks that coefficient, not the remainder in (115). The same Arb/FLINT interval computations certify six displayed scalar decimal values. Exact rational rounding or truncation cells are checked; these numerical certificates are not Lean proofs or an asymptotic error-rate theorem.
